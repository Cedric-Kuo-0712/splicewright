# Narrative starting points

Pick the structure that serves the brief and the footage. These are flexible ways to begin an outline, not recipes or required timeline order. A project can combine them or use a different shape.

## Hook, then chronological travel

Open on one legible highlight or question, then return to the departure and follow the trip forward. It suits a journey with a clear change over time. Check that the opening image or line makes sense before its context; use a simple cut back to the start if an effect would distract.

## Day in the life

Use a recognizable start, a few routine or work beats, one change or interruption, and a closing moment. Capture time can help establish the day, but verify it from evidence and keep the story understandable if a moment is missing.

## Thematic or year review

Group moments around a few ideas, people, places, or recurring activities. A date, location, or brief caption can orient the viewer when the timeline intentionally jumps. Use markers for section boundaries; do not imply a continuous event when the sources only show separate occasions.

## Photo beat montage

Build a short sequence from selected stills, organized by a visual progression, repeated motif, or musical phrase. Vary framing or duration when it helps the rhythm; a static hold is often enough. Add motion only where a restrained transform adds information or energy, and keep faces and text inside the frame.

## Voice or live-audio led

Let a useful spoken thought, ambient sound, or performance establish the sequence, then choose pictures that support it. Keep the source and timing of the audio clear. A detached audio item can be trimmed separately for a sound bridge; detached audio and its picture are no longer linked, so coordinate their later moves deliberately. If a sentence has no supporting image, a clear hold can be better than unrelated coverage.

## Outline before assembly

For each section, note its purpose, one or two candidate ranges, and what the viewer should learn or feel. Distinguish the source's capture time from the sequence's story time. Once the outline is coherent, assemble the spine and add only coverage that clarifies a beat. If the material cannot support a proposed beat, change the outline rather than imply unseen events.
